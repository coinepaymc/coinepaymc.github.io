# 1. What Is Amazon?

Amazon is a global e-commerce platform that provides online shopping services to consumers worldwide. Users can browse products through Amazon's website or mobile app, compare prices, place orders, and pay using the payment methods supported by the relevant marketplace.

Common product categories on Amazon include:

- Electronics: laptops, headphones, keyboards, monitors, and other electronic devices.
- Home and kitchen: furniture, kitchen accessories, storage products, and home decor.
- Clothing and accessories: apparel, shoes, bags, and everyday accessories.
- Office supplies: stationery, office equipment, and related supplies.
- Beauty and personal care: skincare products, beauty tools, and personal care items.
- Everyday essentials: household products, pet supplies, and other daily necessities.

Amazon operates marketplaces in various countries and regions, including the United States, Japan, the United Kingdom, and Germany.

Product availability, shipping options, taxes, payment methods, and card requirements may differ between marketplaces. Before placing an order, check which payment methods are supported by the marketplace you are using and whether the product can be delivered to your shipping address.

# 2. What Payment Methods Does Amazon Accept?

Amazon's available payment methods may vary depending on the country or region, marketplace, order type, and account circumstances. The payment options displayed during checkout are the best way to determine which methods are available for your specific order.

## 1. Credit Cards and Debit Cards

Credit cards and debit cards are common payment methods on many Amazon marketplaces.

If your card is supported by the relevant marketplace and your card issuer authorizes the transaction, you can select an existing card at checkout or add a new one according to the instructions provided.

When adding a card, you may need to provide the following information:

- Card Number: Your card number.
- Expiration Date: The card's expiration date.
- Cardholder Name: The name of the cardholder.
- Billing Address: The address associated with the payment card.
- Additional verification information, if required.

Supported card types, issuing countries, and transaction requirements may differ between marketplaces. Even if your card works on other websites, this does not necessarily mean it will be accepted for every Amazon payment.

## 2. Amazon Gift Cards

Amazon gift cards can be used toward eligible purchases on the applicable Amazon marketplace.

Before using a gift card, keep the following points in mind:

- Gift cards are generally subject to the terms of the applicable country or marketplace.
- Gift cards issued for different Amazon marketplaces may not be interchangeable.
- If the gift card balance is insufficient, another supported payment method may be required to pay the remaining amount.
- Redemption, balance usage, and validity are subject to the relevant marketplace's rules.

If you already have an Amazon gift card, confirm which marketplace it applies to and follow the instructions in your account to redeem or use it.

## 3. Debit Cards and Other Local Payment Methods

Some Amazon marketplaces may support specific local payment methods or other payment services.

Availability depends on your location, order requirements, and Amazon's policies. Not every payment method is available in every country or for every order.

Before submitting your order, review the payment options displayed during checkout.

## 4. Virtual Credit Cards

A virtual credit card is a payment tool that provides card details in digital form and can be used for eligible online transactions according to its terms of service.

For users who frequently purchase products from overseas retailers or need to manage international online spending, a virtual credit card may be an alternative payment option.

Before using one, check the following:

- Whether the card supports international online payments.
- Whether the card issuer permits transactions on the relevant Amazon marketplace.
- Whether the available balance or credit limit is sufficient.
- Whether the billing address meets verification requirements.
- Whether the issuer restricts particular merchants, countries, or transaction types.
- Whether additional identity verification or security checks are required.

**Important: Using a virtual credit card does not guarantee successful payment on Amazon.** Review the card's supported transactions, fees, funding methods, refund policies, and restrictions before deciding whether to use it.

# 3. How to Add a Credit Card to Amazon

If you want to pay for Amazon orders using a bank card or an eligible virtual credit card, follow these general steps.

## Step 1: Sign In to Your Amazon Account

Open the Amazon marketplace you want to use and sign in to your account.

Make sure you are accessing the correct country or regional marketplace and that your shipping address is accurate.

If you are shopping on Amazon US with a card issued in another country or region, check whether the marketplace supports that card and payment arrangement.

## Step 2: Open Your Payment Methods

After signing in, navigate to your account settings or payment methods page.

Depending on the options displayed, select the option to add a payment card or manage your existing payment methods.

Amazon's interface may vary by marketplace, device, and account version. Follow the instructions shown on the page you are using.

## Step 3: Enter Your Card Information

Enter the required card details according to the instructions.

You may need to provide:

- Card number.
- Expiration date.
- Cardholder name.
- Billing address.
- Other required information.

Review the information carefully before saving it.

Make sure you use accurate, authorized card details. Do not use a false cardholder identity or fabricated billing address to bypass payment verification.

## Step 4: Check Your Billing Address

A Billing Address is the address associated with the payment card and may be used to verify transaction information.

A Shipping Address is where your purchased products will be delivered.

These addresses serve different purposes and do not necessarily have to be the same.

If Amazon requests a billing address, enter the actual information registered with your card issuer. Depending on the form, this may include:

- Street address.
- City.
- State or province, if applicable.
- Postal or ZIP code.
- Country or region.

If the billing address does not match the issuer's records, card verification or payment may fail.

## Step 5: Save and Confirm Your Payment Method

Submit the card details and complete any required verification steps.

Some transactions may require additional security checks, depending on the card issuer and transaction circumstances.

Successfully adding a card does not guarantee that every subsequent payment will be approved. Actual transactions may still be affected by the order amount, card status, marketplace rules, and payment authorization results.

# 5. Amazon Payment Declined: How to Fix a Rejected Credit Card

When shopping on Amazon, you may encounter messages such as:

- Payment Declined.
- Payment Failed.
- Payment Revision.
- Please Update Your Payment Method.

These messages may be related to card status, payment information, transaction restrictions, or platform verification.

## 1. Your Card Does Not Support the Transaction

Some bank cards support ordinary online purchases but may not support particular merchants, transaction currencies, or cross-border payments.

Possible reasons include:

- The card issuer restricts the relevant transaction.
- The card does not meet the payment requirements of the marketplace.
- International online payments have not been enabled.
- The card is frozen, deactivated, or expired.
- The transaction does not meet the card's usage conditions.

**Solutions:**

1. Check whether the card is valid and active.
2. Confirm that it supports international online payments.
3. Review the payment methods available during Amazon checkout.
4. Contact the card issuer to ask why the transaction was declined.
5. If the card does not support the transaction, choose another payment method accepted by Amazon.

Avoid repeatedly attempting payment within a short period before identifying the cause of the rejection.

## 2. Insufficient Card Balance

If you are using a prepaid virtual credit card, an insufficient balance is one possible reason for payment failure.

When calculating the amount you need, consider the order total and any additional costs, including:

- Product prices.
- Shipping charges.
- Applicable taxes.
- Currency conversion fees.
- Other fees charged by the card issuer.
- Funds temporarily held for pending transactions.

**Solutions:**

Check the final order total and your available card balance before submitting the payment.

If the balance is sufficient but the payment still fails, investigate card restrictions, billing address information, and the transaction authorization result provided by the issuer.

## 3. Billing Address Mismatch

A billing address is an important verification detail for some card transactions.

If the address entered does not match the information registered with the card issuer, the transaction may be declined.

Common problems include:

- An incomplete address.
- An incorrect city or state.
- An incorrect postal or ZIP code.
- A country or region mismatch.
- An address that differs from the issuer's records.

**Solutions:**

Verify the actual billing address registered with your card issuer and enter it according to Amazon's requirements.

If you are unsure which address is registered, contact your card issuer for confirmation.

Remember that your billing address and shipping address can be different, but both should contain accurate information.

## 4. International Payments Are Not Enabled

Some bank cards require international payment functionality to be activated or specific transaction permissions to be enabled through the bank.

If international online payments are not authorized, or the card has per-transaction or daily spending limits, Amazon payments may fail.

**Solutions:**

Contact your card issuer to confirm:

- Whether the card supports international online payments.
- Whether payments to the relevant merchant are permitted.
- Whether per-transaction or daily limits apply.
- Whether additional security verification is required.
- Whether the transaction was declined by the issuer's risk management system.

If the card issuer confirms that the card does not support the transaction, choose another payment method permitted by Amazon.

## 5. Amazon Has Triggered a Security Review

Amazon may review transactions based on account information, order details, and payment activity.

Examples include:

- Making a first payment with a new account.
- Attempting payment repeatedly within a short period.
- Being asked to verify account or order information.
- Inconsistencies in order details.
- A transaction requiring additional security verification.

These situations do not necessarily mean that your account has violated Amazon's policies. A failed payment alone is not enough to determine the exact cause.

**Solutions:**

First, check whether Amazon has asked you to update your payment information or complete additional verification.

If your account or order status appears unusual, contact Amazon's official customer support. Do not use false information to bypass verification or repeatedly recreate the same order to work around a payment problem.

## 6. The Card Issuer Declined the Transaction

Even when Amazon's website is functioning normally, the card issuer may reject a transaction because of risk controls, spending limits, or other restrictions.

**Solutions:**

Review your card transaction history to determine whether the payment was declined, remains pending, or has already resulted in a charge.

If the card issuer did not receive the transaction request, you may need to investigate Amazon's checkout process or the payment processing stage.

Avoid submitting another payment until you have confirmed the final status of the first transaction.

# 6. What to Do When an Amazon Payment Fails

If your Amazon payment fails, follow these troubleshooting steps in order.

### Step 1: Record the Error Message

Save the complete error message displayed by Amazon, such as Payment Declined, Payment Failed, or Payment Revision.

Different messages may require different solutions.

### Step 2: Check Your Card Information

Verify the card number, expiration date, cardholder information, and other required details. Make sure the card has not expired or been frozen.

### Step 3: Check Your Balance and Transaction Limits

Confirm that your available balance is sufficient. Review per-transaction limits, daily limits, and international payment permissions.

### Step 4: Verify Your Billing Address

Make sure the billing address matches the information registered with your card issuer.

### Step 5: Review Your Transaction History

Check the transaction records provided by your issuer to determine whether the payment was declined, is still being processed, or has resulted in a charge or temporary authorization.

### Step 6: Contact the Appropriate Support Team

If you cannot identify the cause, contact your card issuer or Amazon's official customer support, depending on the nature of the problem.

Providing the exact error message, order status, and transaction time may help resolve the issue more quickly.

### Step 7: Confirm the Transaction Status Before Retrying

If the transaction is still pending, do not immediately submit another payment.

If the order has not been confirmed but funds appear to be unavailable, first determine whether the amount is subject to a temporary authorization or pending transaction. Contact your card issuer if you need clarification about how the funds will be handled.
