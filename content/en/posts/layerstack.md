## 4. How to Choose the Right Cloud Server

You don't always need the most powerful server available. The best option depends on your workload, budget, and future plans.

**1. Choose based on your use case**

- **Personal blogs and small websites:** Focus on basic computing resources, reliability, and affordability.
- **Business websites and online applications:** Consider CPU performance, memory, networking, and scalability.
- **Databases and memory-intensive applications:** Pay close attention to RAM, storage performance, and data access requirements.
- **AI and high-performance computing:** Check whether the CPU, GPU, and software environment meet your specific requirements.

**2. Set a realistic budget**

Determine your monthly or annual budget before choosing a plan. In addition to the server price, check for potential charges related to storage, traffic, backups, and other resources.

**3. Select an appropriate deployment region**

Choose a region close to your target users or connected services whenever possible. This can help reduce network latency. If your audience is spread across multiple countries, test network performance from the locations that matter most.

**4. Consider future scalability**

If you expect your website or application to grow, check how easily you can upgrade CPU, memory, storage, or network resources. Choosing a suitable starting configuration can help avoid unnecessary costs while leaving room for growth.

**5. Compare real-world performance**

Product names alone do not guarantee performance. Review official specifications and relevant test results, and evaluate each option against your application's requirements. If possible, start with a small deployment before committing to a larger configuration.

## 5. Cloud Server Security and Maintenance Tips

- **Protect your login credentials:** Use strong passwords and keep SSH keys and account credentials secure.
- **Keep your system updated:** Install security updates regularly to reduce exposure to known vulnerabilities.
- **Configure a firewall:** Open only the ports required by your applications and restrict unnecessary public access.
- **Back up your data:** Create a backup schedule based on the importance of your data, and periodically test recovery.
- **Monitor resource usage:** Track CPU, memory, disk, and network utilization so you can identify bottlenecks early.
- **Understand billing rules:** Review renewal, cancellation, and additional resource charges to avoid unexpected expenses.
